GENUINE ONE-PACKET OBSTRUCTION TO PURE QUARTIC IMAGINARY CONTRACTION
2026-10-21. Exact analytic derivation; not a Lean certificate.

Scope
-----
The scalar theorem b*dK/db >= 0 does NOT extend unchanged to the
full quartic weighted energy. The example below uses one actual
admissible Gaussian packet, hence its neutral Fourier derivatives are
genuine linked jets. It does not use independent artificial jet data.
It disproves a sign assertion only. It does NOT disprove a uniform
lower bound b*E_b >= -C E, the proposed joint field inequality, or the
user's full physical response estimate.

1. Packet and original positive integral
---------------------------------------
Use beta=1, alpha=32, a=1, L=3-2a=1, and one charged width w=1.
Thus beta>0 and alpha>=32 beta hold exactly. Take

 f(zeta)=exp(-(zeta-1)^2+i zeta)
        =exp(-1)*exp(-zeta^2+(2+i)zeta).

In the original admissible notation this is c=exp(-1), w=1,
neutral parameter B=2+i, and polynomial P=1. The charged amplitude
c*w is therefore the required one. With r=1+i b,

 z=(alpha/2)[r^2(u^2+v^2)+conj(r)^2 Y],
 zeta=x+i 2bv,
 V(X)=1+X^2/2+X^4/4,

consider exactly

 E(b)=(pi/4) integral_{Y>0,u,v,x real}
             V(x+v) |exp(-z) f(x+i2bv)|^2 dx dv du dY.       (1)

Here a and L are fixed when differentiating b, as required for the
imaginary-shrink partial derivative in the new real-dilation split.

The squared charged modulus is
 exp(-32(1-b^2)(u^2+v^2+Y)).
The squared neutral modulus is
 exp(-2(x-1)^2+8b^2 v^2-4bv).
These identities check both the factor2 in the imaginary neutral
shift and the factor alpha/2 in z. Define

 h=32(1-b^2), H=32-40b^2.

For |b|<1/2 both h,H are strictly positive. All integrals and their
b derivatives on smaller closed intervals converge absolutely by a
fixed Gaussian majorant times a polynomial. Differentiation under
the integrals and Gaussian evaluation are therefore justified.

2. Exact Gaussian evaluation
----------------------------
The u,Y integrals contribute sqrt(pi)*h^(-3/2).
Completing the v square gives a Gaussian with

 mean_v=-2b/H, variance_v=1/(2H),
 integral_v exp(-Hv^2-4bv)=sqrt(pi/H)*exp(4b^2/H).

The x Gaussian has mean1, variance1/4, and mass sqrt(pi/2).
Thus the physical field x+v has mean and variance

 M=1-2b/H, s=1/4+1/(2H).

Set
 Q(M,s)=1+(M^2+s)/2+(M^4+6M^2s+3s^2)/4.
The exact result, with no b-dependent constant omitted, is

 E(b)=C h^(-3/2) H^(-1/2) exp(4b^2/H) Q(M,s),              (2)
 C=pi^(5/2)/(4 sqrt(2))>0.

3. Exact sign at and near zero
-----------------------------
At b=0, h=H=32, M=1, s=17/64. Their derivatives satisfy
 h'=H'=s'=0, M'=-1/16, and (4b^2/H)'=0. Direct rational arithmetic
now gives

 Q(1,17/64)=38243/16384,
 Q_M(1,17/64)=179/64,
 E'(0)/E(0)=(-1/16)*(179/64)/(38243/16384)
           =-2864/38243 < 0.                               (3)

Hence continuity gives b E'(b)<0 at all sufficiently small b>0.
No statement is made that b E'(b) is negative at b=0 itself: it is
zero there because of the exterior factor b.

An explicit rational parameter also works: b=1/100. The logarithmic
derivative of (2) is the following rational expression in b:

 D(b)=-(3/2)h'/h-(1/2)H'/H+(4b^2/H)'
       +(Q_M M'+Q_s s')/Q.

At b=1/100, exact substitution and rational simplification give

 D(1/100)=
 -3842942777341457053850380400
 /130327818267455420279324597391 < 0.                        (4)

All factors in E(1/100) are positive, so (4) proves
(1/100) E'(1/100)<0 without a floating-point sign test. The algebra
in (3) is a smaller independently checkable certificate; (4) selects
one concrete nonzero b rather than relying only on continuity.

4. Compatibility with the actual clock strip
--------------------------------------------
The point a=1,b=1/100 can also occur on the actual clock, rather than
only in an enlarged parameter rectangle. Set epsilon=1 and, for any
0<b<=1/100,

 R=1+b^2, d=log R,
 q=d/b^2>0, real_clock_s=log q,
 theta=2 arctan b,
 y=theta+2d/b.

Then r^2=1-b^2+i2b and
 complex_inverse_candidate=real_clock_s+d+i theta.
Its exponential is q r^2. Consequently

 exp(complex_inverse_candidate)+complex_inverse_candidate
 = [q+real_clock_s] + i y,

because q b^2=d. Its real part is precisely the real-clock chart at
real_clock_s, and its imaginary part is y. Moreover
0<y<=4b<=1/25<pi/3, by arctan b<=b and log(1+b^2)<=b^2.
The candidate is in the same inverse strip, so uniqueness of the
clock inverse gives relative scale exp((d+i theta)/2)=1+i b.
The associated normalized fraction is t=d/(b^2+d).
Thus this obstruction occurs at admissible clock points as well.
It still concerns only the fixed-a partial b derivative; the other
terms in the actual total derivative must not be dropped.

5. What remains open
--------------------
This negative first derivative comes from the shift of the physical
field mean M=1-2b/H. The scalar growth and all even Gaussian-width
terms have zero first derivative at b=0, whereas the quartic field
moment has the nonzero linear derivative in (3). That mechanism is
absent from the scalar channel.

The example does not show arbitrarily negative relative derivatives.
Indeed (3)-(4) have finite relative derivatives. A valid next theorem
may be a finite uniform defect bound for b E_b, or a joint inequality
including the small field motion in the real-dilation decomposition.
