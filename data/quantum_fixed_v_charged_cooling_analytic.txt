FIXED-v CHARGED COOLING: ANALYTIC POSITIVITY PROOF
2026-10-21. Analytic argument, not a Lean-verified theorem.

Statement and scope
-------------------
Let alpha>0, a>0, b real, and a>|b|. Put r=a+i b,
p=(alpha/2)r^2, p_a=alpha r, and theta=arg(p)=2 arg(r).
Then |theta|<pi/2 and arg(p_a)=theta/2. For any finite list of
positive real widths w_i and any real v define

 S_ij=p w_i+conj(p) w_j,  T_ij=conj(S_ij),
 Sdot_ij=p_a w_i+conj(p_a) w_j, Tdot_ij=conj(Sdot_ij),
 K_v,ij=exp(-S_ij v^2) S_ij^(-1/2) T_ij^(-1).

The square root is the principal one. The matrices K_v are positive
semidefinite, and -partial_a K_v is positive semidefinite, with b and
v held fixed. This holds for every finite family, without a separation
condition on the widths or a numerical conditioning cutoff. The widths
need only be positive, not at least one.

Equivalently, increasing the real part of r with b fixed decreases the
charged norm after integration in u in R and Y>0, at every fixed v.
The u,Y integral has the additional harmless constant sqrt(pi).
Transposing the convention for S, as in some repository formulas,
conjugates these Hermitian matrices and preserves positivity.

This statement concerns the derivative of the charged kernel while its
coefficient vector is frozen. The vector can be any function of v and
of neutral Fourier frequency. Consequently this proves the charged
part of cooling for arbitrary quadratic neutral jets, whenever the
already established integrability justifies the integrals. It does not
by itself bound the additional derivative of the field multiplier
nu=L-i eta, nor prove the full uniform quartic-response estimate.

1. A scalar normalized shifted-cosh positivity criterion
-------------------------------------------------------
For |Im z|<pi/2 there is a single analytic logarithm L(z)=log(cosh z)
with L(0)=0 and real L(x) for real x. Indeed Re(cosh(x+i y))=
cosh(x)cos(y)>0 on this strip, so this is the principal logarithm.
Its elementary integral representation is

 L(z)=integral_0^infinity (1-cos(2zt))/(t sinh(pi t)) dt.          (1)

The integral is locally uniformly convergent in the strip. One direct
verification uses 1/sinh(pi t)=2 sum_{n>=0} exp(-(2n+1)pi t),
the elementary Laplace-log integral, and the Euler product
cosh z=product_{n>=0}(1+4z^2/((2n+1)^2 pi^2)). This first proves
(1) for real z and then throughout the strip by analytic continuation.
No branch choice outside this strip is used.

Suppose real shifts lambda_j and lambda_0 lie in (-pi/2,pi/2),
real exponents gamma_j are fixed, and set

 k(x)=[cosh(x+i lambda_0)/cos(lambda_0)]
      product_j [cos(lambda_j)/cosh(x+i lambda_j)]^gamma_j.

Powers use L. Define, for t>0,
 A(t)=sum_j gamma_j cosh(2 lambda_j t)-cosh(2 lambda_0 t),
 B(t)=sum_j gamma_j sinh(2 lambda_j t)-sinh(2 lambda_0 t).
Then (1) gives exactly

 log k(x)=integral_0^infinity
   [A(t)(cos(2xt)-1)-i B(t)sin(2xt)]/(t sinh(pi t)) dt.         (2)

If both A(t)+B(t) and A(t)-B(t) are nonnegative, define

 nu_plus(t) =(A(t)-B(t))/(2t sinh(pi t)),
 nu_minus(t)=(A(t)+B(t))/(2t sinh(pi t)).

For each epsilon>0 these are finite positive measures on
(epsilon,infinity), because all shifts lie inside the strip.
The exponential of the integral over that interval in (2) is

 k_epsilon(x)=exp integral_epsilon^infinity
   [(exp(2ixt)-1)nu_plus(t)+(exp(-2ixt)-1)nu_minus(t)] dt.

This is a compound-Poisson characteristic function. Alternatively,
its positive definiteness follows directly by expanding the exponential
of the Fourier transform of a finite positive measure and taking
Schur products. Near zero A(t)=O(1), B(t)=O(t), so the paired
integrand in (2) is O(1). The improper integral therefore converges
and k_epsilon(x) tends pointwise to k(x). Each finite Gram matrix
converges entrywise; its quadratic form remains nonnegative. Thus k
is positive definite on R. The truncation treats both signs together;
no missing or unspecified linear compensation is present.

2. Positivity of B_kernel=Sdot S^(-1/2) T^(-1)
------------------------------------------------
Write x_i=(1/2)log w_i and x=x_i-x_j. Set c0=2|p|>0 and
c1=2|p_a|>0. Then

 S_ij=c0 sqrt(w_i w_j) cosh(x+i theta),
 Sdot_ij=c1 sqrt(w_i w_j) cosh(x+i theta/2).

Therefore B_kernel is a positive constant times a positive diagonal
congruence of the matrix k_B(x_i-x_j), where

 k_B(x)=[cosh(x+i theta/2)/cos(theta/2)]
        [cos(theta)/cosh(x+i theta)]^(1/2)
        [cos(theta)/cosh(x-i theta)].

More precisely the constant is
 c1 c0^(-3/2) cos(theta/2) cos(theta)^(-3/2),
and the congruence diagonal is w_i^(-1/4).
For this ratio,
 A=3/2 cosh(2theta t)-cosh(theta t),
 B=-1/2 sinh(2theta t)-sinh(theta t).
Consequently A+B and A-B have the forms

 (1/2) exp(2theta t)+exp(-2theta t)-exp(theta t)

and the same expression with t replaced by -t. For any x>0,

 x^2[(1/2)x^2+x^(-2)-x]
 = (1/2)x^4-x^3+1
 = (x-3/2)^2[(1/2)x^2+(1/2)x+3/8]+5/32 > 0.               (3)

Apply (3) with x=exp(theta t), or its reciprocal. The criterion in
section 1 proves k_B positive definite, hence B_kernel is positive
semidefinite.

3. Positivity of C_kernel=Tdot S^(-1/2) T^(-2)
------------------------------------------------
The same diagonal-congruence reduction now gives

 k_C(x)=[cosh(x-i theta/2)/cos(theta/2)]
        [cos(theta)/cosh(x+i theta)]^(1/2)
        [cos(theta)/cosh(x-i theta)]^2.

The constant is c1 c0^(-5/2) cos(theta/2) cos(theta)^(-5/2),
and the congruence diagonal is w_i^(-3/4). The two candidate Levy
densities have numerators

 (1/2) exp(2theta t)+2 exp(-2theta t)-exp(-theta t)

and its reflected version. For x>0 their positivity follows from

 x^2[(1/2)x^2+2x^(-2)-x^(-1)]
 = (1/2)x^4-x+2
 = (1/2)(x-1)^2(x^2+2x+3)+x+1/2 > 0.                    (4)

Thus k_C, and hence C_kernel, are positive semidefinite.

4. The derivative is a sum of positive Schur products
----------------------------------------------------
Entrywise differentiation, with the square-root branch already fixed,
gives the exact identity

 -partial_a K_v
 = exp(-S v^2) * [v^2 B_kernel +(1/2) B_kernel * S^(-1)
                  + C_kernel],                           (5)

where * means entrywise product. The matrix exp(-S_ij v^2) is
rank-one positive semidefinite: its entries are
exp(-p w_i v^2) conjugate(exp(-p w_j v^2)). Also

 S_ij^(-1)=integral_0^infinity
              exp(-p w_i t) conjugate(exp(-p w_j t)) dt,

so S^(-1) is positive semidefinite. By sections 2 and 3, every term
on the right side of (5) is positive semidefinite under the Schur
product theorem. This proves the claim. The positivity of K_v itself
also follows by the same Laplace-Gram representation for S^(-1/2)
and T^(-1).

At theta=0 all formulas are regular. The normalized B ratio becomes
cosh(x)^(-1/2), and C becomes cosh(x)^(-3/2). There is no division
by b, theta, or sin(theta). Nothing here covers the boundary
|theta|=pi/2, where the original u,Y integrals also cease to have
the required strict exponential decay.

Verification status
-------------------
This is a self-contained analytic derivation checked independently by
the response-review and Hamiltonian-domains agents. It is not a Lean
proof and is outside the Lean theorem audit. The finite
confluent-width diagnostics preceded this argument and are not needed
for its validity. The full quartic theorem still requires control of
the moving field coefficients, using or compatible with (5).
